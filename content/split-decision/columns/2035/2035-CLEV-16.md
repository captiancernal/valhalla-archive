## Mickey

Kenny says I'm not allowed to print the mark. Fine. I didn't print it. It's in my notebook. It'll stay there.

## Ken

A three-point loss to undefeated Miami (OH) in the national championship game. Fourteen and two.

Mickey has asked me to describe The Thing. I won't. I've watched it eleven times. I can't tell you, as a matter of fact, what happened. I can only tell you what it felt like, which is that something that should have been one way went another way, and that a press box full of reporters from Cleveland went very quiet and stayed quiet for a long time. That's not analysis. That's the only honest thing I have.

Nadia called me. She was crying. She said, "Ken, I didn't want it like that." I said, "I know." She said, "Come back Tuesday." I said, "I'll come back Tuesday." I will.

Mickey and I walked out of the stadium together. It was cold. He said, "Kenny, I think I'm done." I said, "You've said that before." He said, "I know. This time I'm going to sleep on it." I said, "Sleep on it for a month." He said, "A month." We stood in the parking lot. Then he said, "Seventy points, Kenny. We scored seventy." I said, "I know." He said, "That's a lot of points to lose with." I said, "That's the most points anyone's ever lost with." He laughed. It was a small laugh. It was enough.
