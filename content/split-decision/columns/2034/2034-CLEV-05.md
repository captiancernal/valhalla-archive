## Mickey

Vikings 56, Fresno State 9, in Fresno. Five and oh.

A brand-new opponent, folks: the Bulldogs, out in California's Central Valley. Forty-seven points of daylight. I've never been to Fresno. I'm told it's hot and full of almonds. Our guys went out there, ate the almonds and came home.

The defense allowed nine. Five games, forty-seven points allowed. I said last week I'd need a thesaurus for this defense. I bought one. The words in it for "stingy" include "parsimonious," "miserly" and "close-fisted." Big Stan read the list and said, "Mick, just call them cheap." So: our defense is cheap. In the best way.

Sofia Reyes is fourteen now, a freshman in high school, and this fall she's the statistician for Leo's varsity team. Official. With a headset. She sits in the press box at the high school, keeps every number in her clipboard and a laptop, and radios down to the coaches. Leo is a junior now, starting at cornerback. Sofia keeps his stats too. She says he's "statistically above average, personally annoying." That's a sister.

Marco sends me her stat sheets every Friday night. I read them on Saturday mornings before our game. Her handwriting is better than mine. Her analysis is better than Kenny's. Don't tell Kenny.

Four pierogi. One on the plate.

## Ken

A forty-seven-point road win at Fresno State, a first-time opponent. Five and oh. The Vikings have allowed forty-seven points total in five games. I've double-checked. That's correct.

Sofia Reyes is a varsity statistician at fourteen. Mickey says her analysis is better than mine. I've read three of her stat sheets. Mickey is correct. She tracks something I've never tracked: "plays where Leo was in the right place but nobody threw at him." She calls it "invisible coverage." It's brilliant. It measures the thing that doesn't happen because someone was ready. I've added a version to the Doom Model. I've credited her by name.

When I told her, through Mickey, she sent back a note: "Mr. Ken, you can use it. But you have to call it the Sofia Stat." So it's the Sofia Stat. It's the best column in my spreadsheet.

Teresa and I ran eight miles on Saturday morning before the game. Eight. I've never done anything for eight miles except worry. She says I'm "almost there." I asked again where "there" is. She said, "Kenny, it's the half marathon in April." I see. "There" is thirteen miles. My wife tells me the destination only after I've committed to the trip.

## Back and forth

## Final Verdict

A first trip to Fresno for a forty-seven-point win, a defense best described as cheap, and a fourteen-year-old statistician who invented a stat for things that don't happen.
