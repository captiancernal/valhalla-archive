## Mickey

A road win in Orlando in late November, friends. Vikings 34, UCF 27. I could get used to Florida, except I'd miss the lake, the pierogi, and complaining about the lake.

That's 7-4, my people. Seven wins. Do you know how many Cleveland State football seasons I've covered where seven wins would've gotten somebody a statue? I'll tell you: all of them. And here's the thing I keep coming back to: this team keeps winning *one-score* road games. Tulane, SMU, now UCF. That's not luck anymore. That's a habit, and habits are what champions are made of.

The second seat traveled with me this week. I gave it to a Vikings fan I met at the hotel, a retired pipefitter named Bernard who drove down from Akron "just to see if this was real." Bernard, it's real.

Three pierogi, eaten cold out of a hotel mini-fridge. I've never been happier.

## Ken

Seven wins. I'm running out of ways to be unpleasant about it, and I resent that.

A seven-point road win at UCF is a legitimately good result. That's the third one-score road win of the season, and Mickey's right that it's starting to look like a skill rather than a lucky charm. A team that keeps winning close road games has learned something about itself, and teams that learn things are, statistically, more annoying to doubt.

However. The regular season ends in Oxford, against a Miami (OH) team that treats defense like an optional elective. Joe Snyderwine's RedHawks will score. The only question is whether the Vikings can match them, and "can you win a track meet on the road" is not a question I enjoy answering in pencil.

The Doom Model has downgraded this season from "doomed" to "doomed, eventually." That's the most optimistic it's ever been.
