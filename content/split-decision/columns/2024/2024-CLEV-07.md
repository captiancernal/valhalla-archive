## Mickey

Colorado 24, Vikings 21. On the road, in the mountains, by three.

I went to Sal's for a haircut Monday morning, which was a mistake. Sal Moretti has cut what's left of my hair for twenty-five years. Sal was born in Athens, Ohio, and bleeds Bobcat green, and Sal had the radio on and a look on his face like a man who's been waiting all week.

"Close losses build character, Mick," he said, clippers buzzing. "Ask Ohio. We've got so much character we had to rent a storage unit." Then he took a little more off the sides than I asked for, and I'm almost certain it was on purpose.

Here's the honest thing: we were close and we didn't finish. We're 4-3. Our three losses are by eleven, four and three. That's a team knocking on the door. Teams that knock on the door this many times eventually get invited in for coffee.

Zero pierogi. A salad tonight, out of solidarity, and a hat until the sides grow back.

## Ken

Mickey is eating a salad. I want that on the record. That's how you know it's bad.

Three losses, all by one or two scores. The Vikings are 4-3, and every loss has the same shape: competitive, respectable and wrong at the end. You can call that bad luck once. Three times, it's a profile.

I came home and put on a ballad record so slow the needle nearly fell asleep. My neighbor knocked to ask if everything was okay. I said, "Colorado." He said, "Ah." In Cleveland, that's a full conversation.

The honest version: a first-year staff in a new conference is three possessions away from being undefeated. Either that's a good team that hasn't learned to close, or a mediocre one that keeps getting lucky enough to be close. The Doom Model believes the second. I believe the Doom Model, mostly.

Kansas comes to town next. If the Vikings can't handle that at home, I'll write the "regression" column myself. I already have a draft. It's long.

## Final Verdict

Close again, short again, and the only thing that got shut down more efficiently than the offense was Mickey's hairline.
