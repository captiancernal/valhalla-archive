## Mickey

Colorado 24, Vikings 21. On the road. In the mountains. Three points.

You know, friends, I've noticed a pattern this season and I don't like it. When we win close, I write about grit. When we lose close, I write about altitude. I'm going to try something new this week. I'm going to just say it: we were close and we didn't finish. That's on all of us, including me, because I wore my lucky cardigan inside out and I think the universe noticed.

We're 4-3. Our three losses are by eleven, four and three. That's a team that's knocking on the door. A team that knocks on the door this many times eventually gets invited in for coffee.

The second seat went to a firefighter named Theresa who said close losses are "just practice for close wins." I'm stealing that and putting it on the fridge.

Zero pierogi. I'm eating a salad tonight out of solidarity.

## Ken

Mickey is eating a salad. I want that on the record. That's how you know it's bad.

Three losses, all by one or two scores. The Vikings are 4-3 and every loss has the same shape: competitive, respectable, and wrong at the end. You can call that bad luck once. Three times, it's a profile.

But I'm not going to pile on, because the honest version of this column is that a first-year staff in a new conference is three possessions away from being undefeated. That's either a sign of a good team that hasn't learned to close, or a mediocre team that keeps getting lucky enough to be close. The Doom Model believes the second one. I believe the Doom Model, mostly.

Kansas comes to town next. If the Vikings can't handle that one at home, I'll write the "regression" column myself. I already have a draft. It's long.
