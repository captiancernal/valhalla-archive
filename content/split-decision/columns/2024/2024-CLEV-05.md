## Mickey

I'm writing this one with my reading glasses pushed up on my head, which Donna says is my "sad position."

Texas Tech 17, Vikings 13. On the road, in Lubbock, in front of a crowd that sounded like a jet engine with opinions. We're 3-2, and I want to be honest with you, my people: it hurt. Four points. One touchdown. One play, probably, somewhere in there, that bounces the other way and I'm writing about pierogi instead of my feelings.

But I'll tell you what I saw. I saw a team that didn't quit in a hostile building. I saw a defense that held a Big 12 offense to seventeen, which after the forty-twos is like watching your kid finally clean his room. That's growth, friends.

The second seat went to Marisol, a bus driver who drives the night route. She said, "Some nights the road wins." Then she bought me a pretzel. Zero pierogi. One pretzel. Life goes on.

## Ken

I don't enjoy being right. That's a lie; I enjoy it a little. But not like this.

Thirteen points. After scoring thirty-one, forty-five and twenty-eight, the offense went to West Texas and forgot to pack. And you know what? The defense played its best game of the year, which is the most Cleveland sentence I've ever typed: when one half finally shows up, the other half stays home.

Mickey wants to celebrate seventeen points allowed. I'd like to remind him the scoreboard keeps track of both teams. It's very thorough.

Here's the part I'll grudgingly add: losing by four on the road to Texas Tech is not a disaster. It's a normal, annoying, Big 12 Saturday. The Doom Model predicted a loss, and the Doom Model would like that noted in the official minutes.

## Back and forth

**Mickey:** The defense showed up, Kenny!

**Ken:** And the offense went to the wrong stadium. Lubbock has two, I assume.

**Mickey:** It's four points. Four!

**Ken:** Seventeen is more than thirteen. I have a degree in this.

## Final Verdict

A gutsy road loss, a defense with a pulse, and an offense that owes the rest of the team a pretzel.
