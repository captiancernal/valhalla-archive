## Mickey

The Vikings played on Halloween this year, in Orlando, and I wasn't home to hand out candy for the first time in twenty-nine years. So I asked the Reyes kids to do it for me.

Donna sent pictures. Leo, eight, was a Viking for the third straight year, horns slightly dented now. Sofia, six, went as Big Stan, apron, little paper hat, a cardboard salami, and she apparently stood on my porch telling every trick-or-treater, "One per customer," in a voice that I'm told was an alarmingly good impression. Stan has asked for a framed copy. Stan cried.

Meanwhile, down in Florida, our guys beat UCF 38-24. Third straight road win. Fourteen points of daylight. Seven and one, folks. Three road games into a five-game trip and we haven't lost a step. You know what that tells me? This team packs well.

Four pierogi. One per customer. Sofia's rules.

## Ken

The Vikings beat UCF by two touchdowns on Halloween, which is appropriate, because this road trip is starting to feel spooky in the opposite way. Nothing is going wrong. I keep checking the closets.

Three road wins in a row: Lubbock, Oxford, Orlando. I've been doing this a long time, and I'll say plainly: a team that can win in three different kinds of building, a loud West Texas stadium, a shootout in Ohio and a muggy Florida night, has a real identity. It's not a fluke team. It's a road team.

I spent Halloween at the Lantern Room. Nadia dressed as a RedHawk, wings and all, and refused to serve me unless I said "caw." I said "caw." It was quiet and dignified. Regression to the Mean finished last in the costume-themed trivia round, because nobody on my team could name a single famous monster. We're numbers people. Our monsters are spreadsheets.

Two to go on the trip: Utah and Arizona. Arizona beat us at home last year by three touchdowns. I have a calendar with red marks. I've been using the red marks less lately.
