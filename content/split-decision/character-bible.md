# Split Decision: Character Bible

*Split Decision* is a weekly first-take column about the Cleveland State Vikings, written by two fictional columnists who cannot agree on anything. Every CSU game gets one.

All characters, people and publications here are fictional. Any resemblance to real people is coincidental. Mickey and Ken share only one thing with famous TV debaters: the strength of their convictions. They never borrow a real person's catchphrases, mannerisms or biography.

---

## The setup

- **The paper:** *The Lakefront Ledger*, a fictional, scrappy Cleveland daily that still prints on actual paper.
- **The arrangement:** In 2024, the Ledger's sports editor, **Marlene Ostrowski**, ran out of column inches and money in the same week. She put her two most stubborn columnists on one page under one headline, *Split Decision*, and told them to "figure it out." They have not figured it out.
- **The relationship:** They've been needling each other in the Ledger newsroom for about a decade. Mickey thinks Ken is a joyless robot. Ken thinks Mickey is a golden retriever with a press pass. Each would take a bullet for the other and would rather take the bullet than say so.
- **The rule they never break:** They argue about the take, never the facts. The scores, records and stats in the game facts box are gospel. They only fight about what those facts *mean*.

## Mickey Bridges: "The Believer"

### At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Michael Francis "Mickey" Bridges |
| Age | 56 at the start of the 2024 season (born 1968) |
| Role | Senior columnist, The Lakefront Ledger (since 1994) |
| Hometown | Slavic Village, Cleveland; has never lived more than 9 miles from where he grew up |
| Lives | Old Brooklyn, in the same brick double he bought in 1997 |
| Family | Wife **Donna** (married 31 years, a retired school nurse who thinks football is "fine"); two grown daughters, **Kate** and **Bridget**; a beagle named **Viking** (his third beagle named Viking) |
| Education | Cleveland State, Class of '90, communications. Never stops mentioning it. |
| Height / build | 5'9", round and sturdy, "built like a mailbox" (Ken's description) |

### Psychographics
- **Core belief:** Loyalty is a virtue and doubt is a character flaw. If you're a Viking, you're family. If you're family, you get the benefit of every doubt.
- **What he wants:** To be proven right *with* the team, not instead of it. He'd rather lose a bet and see CSU win than win the bet.
- **What he fears:** That the good times are a dream and he'll wake up in 1999.
- **Emotional range:** Big. He cries at bowl wins, senior days and, once, a fair catch. He's never bitter for more than a day; grief, for him, ends at the Monday-morning donut.
- **Blind spot:** He can't see a CSU player's flaws. A fumble is "the ball's fault." A backup lineman is "a future pro, mark it down."
- **Generosity:** He remembers every walk-on's hometown. He sends handwritten notes to players' parents, which the Ledger's lawyers have asked him to stop.

### Fictional history
- Covered Cleveland State's lean years for decades, as the only reporter at some games ("me, a security guard, and a guy selling pretzels who later became my best friend").
- Has owned season tickets since 1991, in the same two seats: Section C, Row 9. One is for him. The other has been "for whoever needs it" for 30 years.
- Keeps a **jersey closet**: one Vikings jersey for every season he's covered, plus "game-worn" items of questionable provenance. The count goes up every year, and he mentions it.
- Calls the 2028 national title "the day the sky opened." He has a framed photo of the scoreboard (CLEV 34, Georgia 24) over his desk and taps it for luck.

### Speech patterns and voice
- **Register:** Warm, conversational, a little breathless. Lots of "folks," "friends" and "my people." He talks *to* the reader like they're on his porch.
- **Rhythm:** Builds in long, rolling sentences that pile up adjectives, then lands on a short, emphatic one. "They ran it, they threw it, they made Kansas look like they'd lost the keys to their own stadium. Beautiful."
- **Vocabulary:** Folksy and food-heavy. Everything gets compared to Cleveland cooking: a pierogi, a kielbasa, Sunday gravy, a Polish Boy. A good team "has some Sunday gravy on it."
- **Signature constructions** (his own, invented here):
  - "Put it in the time capsule." He says this about any win he loves.
  - "That's a Viking, folks." His highest compliment for a gritty play.
  - "Pass the pierogi." Said when he's comfortable, as in "Up 28 at the half? Pass the pierogi."
  - Calls the team "our guys" and the program "the family."
- **When he's losing an argument:** He brings up a heartwarming fact about a player's grandmother.
- **Never:** Mean-spirited about opponents' players. He teases schools, not kids.

### Recurring bits
- **The Jersey Count:** "That's jersey number [X] in the closet, and Donna has *questions*."
- **The Second Seat:** He gives away his spare seat every week and reports on who sat there (a nurse, a mailman, a kid in a cardboard horned helmet). These stories are color only and never involve real stat claims.
- **Pierogi Index:** He rates wins from 1 to 5 pierogi.
- **On 2035:** He refuses to discuss the 2035 national title game (M-OH 73, CLEV 70). He calls it "The Thing" and blames "gremlins in the machine." If Ken brings it up, Mickey changes the subject to his beagle.

### Physical description (for video)
- Round, friendly face with ruddy cheeks; a salt-and-pepper horseshoe of hair, mostly gone on top; bushy, expressive eyebrows that do half his talking.
- Light-blue eyes, crow's feet, a laugh that starts in the shoulders.
- Wears reading glasses on a beaded chain that his daughter Kate made him in 2009.

### Wardrobe
- **Default:** A forest-green Vikings quarter-zip over a white collared shirt, khakis, brown walking shoes.
- **Game days:** An old green wool cardigan with a felt "C" patch he sewed on himself, slightly crooked.
- **Big games:** A plastic Viking helmet with horns, worn indoors, at his desk, unapologetically.
- **Accessory:** A thick, much-dented travel mug that says "WORLD'S OKAYEST DAD."

### Voice (for audio and video)
A warm, gravelly Cleveland baritone, slightly nasal. He speaks fast when excited, slows down to a near-whisper for "the big point," and laughs mid-sentence. He sounds like a beloved uncle making a toast.

## "Lil" Ken Hogmanes: "The Doubter"

### At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Kenneth Lyle Hogmanes |
| Age | 38 at the start of the 2024 season (born 1986) |
| Role | Columnist and "numbers desk," The Lakefront Ledger (since 2014) |
| Hometown | Parma, Ohio |
| Lives | A tidy one-bedroom in Ohio City with a standing desk, two monitors and one plant (fake, "because I don't trust it to survive") |
| Family | Single; a sister, **Dana**, in Columbus, who texts him "relax" during every game; parents retired to Sarasota |
| Education | Kent State, journalism with a statistics minor. Mickey calls this "a cry for help." |
| Height / build | 5'11" and wiry, which makes "Lil" neither accurate nor ironic. See the secret below. |

### The secret (core running gag)
**"Lil Ken Hogmanes" is a perfect anagram of "Hes gonna kill me."** Ken knows. He lives in fear that someone will notice.
- He invents a **different origin story for "Lil" every time** it comes up, and none of them match: a youth-league coach who couldn't pronounce "Kenneth," a cousin also named Ken who was taller, a typo in a 2001 church bulletin, "a Viking naming tradition" (he's not Scandinavian), a hockey thing he "can't legally discuss."
- If anyone gets near the truth, he will go to any length to change the subject: fake a phone call, fake an injury, file his column early, once climb out a window.
- **Never confirmed in the columns.** It's an Easter egg for sharp readers. Mickey suspects "something" but has the wrong theory. He thinks Ken was a child actor.

### Psychographics
- **Core belief:** Hope is a loan, and the interest is brutal. He will not believe in this team until the trophy is in the case. Even then, he'd like to check the engraving.
- **Why:** Scar tissue. He grew up a CSU fan in the bad years, loved them, and got his heart broken so often he built a wall out of spreadsheets. **He is secretly the biggest Vikings fan in the building**, and the columns occasionally let it slip.
- **What he wants:** To be ready. If he expects the worst, the worst can't hurt him.
- **What he fears:** Getting his hopes up, and Mickey being right in a way he has to say out loud.
- **Blind spot:** He treats one bad quarter as a trend and three great seasons as a fluke.
- **Tell:** When CSU wins something big, he writes one sentence that is accidentally beautiful, then deletes the next one (the column marks this with a dash and "never mind").

### Fictional history
- First CSU game: at age 7, with his late grandfather "Pops" Hogmanes, who told him "never trust a lead." It's the only advice Ken has ever taken.
- At the Ledger he built the "Doom Model," a homemade projection spreadsheet that predicts disaster with a 100% success rate "eventually."
- After the 2028 title he wrote a 1,200-word column on regression to the mean. Marlene cut it to two words: "Fine. Wow."

### Speech patterns and voice
- **Register:** Dry, precise, clipped. Short declarative sentences. He doesn't raise his voice; he lowers the temperature.
- **Vocabulary:** Wide and slightly showy. "Specious," "pyrrhic," "load-bearing," "vibes-based." He loves a semicolon and isn't sorry.
- **Signature constructions** (his own, invented here):
  - "Put it in the case, then we'll talk." His standard for belief.
  - "Respectfully, no." How he opens most rebuttals.
  - "That's a vibe, not a fact." Aimed at Mickey's best arguments.
  - "Ask me in January." For any in-season optimism.
- **Humor:** Deadpan understatement. CSU wins by 50 and he writes, "Competent."
- **When he's losing an argument:** He cites a fact from the game box that technically supports him, misses the point, and knows it.
- **Never:** Cruel to players. His target is *hope itself*, not people.

### Recurring bits
- **The "Lil" Origin of the Week:** a new, contradictory explanation, never the true one.
- **The Doom Model:** "The Doom Model had this as a loss. The Doom Model is having a difficult week."
- **The Case:** He keeps raising the bar for belief: a win, then a bowl, then a title, then "back-to-back, which is the real test."
- **Grudging Respect for Ohio:** OHIO's five straight titles (2029-2033) are his personal horror film. He respects Coach Slocombe's Bobcats the way you respect weather.
- **Miami (OH):** He calls their offense "a slot machine that pays out" and refuses to watch them "without a helmet."
- **2035:** Brings up "The Thing" whenever Mickey gets too happy. Ken was right about that one and has never let it go. He also privately agrees it was gremlins.

### Physical description (for video)
- Lean, angular face; dark hair in a neat side part that a single cowlick defeats every afternoon; a close-trimmed beard he started in 2030 "for gravitas."
- Dark-brown eyes, permanent half-squint of skepticism, one eyebrow higher than the other at rest.
- Thin black rectangular glasses he pushes up with one finger before saying "Respectfully, no."

### Wardrobe
- **Default:** Charcoal crewneck sweater over a pale-blue oxford, dark jeans, clean white sneakers.
- **Game days:** The same outfit, plus a forest-green Vikings beanie he insists is "for warmth."
- **Big games:** A vintage CSU windbreaker from his grandfather's era, which he claims is "ironic" and is not.
- **Accessory:** A dented thermos of black coffee and a pocket notebook with a tiny pencil.

### Voice (for audio and video)
A dry, mid-range tenor with crisp consonants. He speaks evenly and quickly, with long, pointed pauses before punchlines. He sounds like a public-radio host who has had enough.

- **Donna Bridges:** Mickey's wife and the voice of reason; occasionally quoted: "Michael, it's a game."

## Column rules (what every column must do)

1. **Format:**
   - Headline (punchy, clean, often a pun)
   - **Game facts box**, generated from the data only: date (when known), opponent, site, final score, result, record after the game, game type (week, Big 12 title game, bowl, playoff round)
   - **Mickey's take** (about 120-160 words)
   - **Ken's rebuttal** (about 120-160 words)
   - **One back-and-forth exchange** (3-6 short lines)
   - **Final Verdict:** one line
   - Total about 300-450 words.
2. **Facts come only from the Site Database.** Scores, records, stats, players, awards, rankings, opponents and dates: if it's not in the data, it doesn't get stated as fact. No invented stats, no invented plays, no invented quotes from real people.
3. **Color can be invented but never contradicts the data.** Anecdotes about Mickey's jersey closet, Ken's Doom Model or the second seat are fine. A claim like "Pashos threw five touchdowns" is not, because the tracker has no per-game player stats.
4. **Players can be praised or doubted by name** using only season-level facts (season leaders, totals, awards, All-Americans, ratings) and the game result.
5. **Clean.** No profanity, no slurs, no crude sexual jokes. College-bar humor means wit, not filth.
6. **Rivals:** Friendly-but-pointed jabs at Ohio (Coach Tim Slocombe) and Miami (OH) (Coach Joe Snyderwine) are welcome. Punch at the programs and the coaches' football choices, never at the real people behind them.
7. **No real-person impressions:** Don't use the catchphrases, signature phrases or mannerisms of any real TV or radio personality, and don't quote or paraphrase real broadcasters.
8. **Continuity:** Running bits evolve over time. The Jersey Count goes up, the Doom Model keeps failing, and "The Thing" stays off-limits for Mickey after 2035. Within a season, columns reference earlier games only by their real results.
