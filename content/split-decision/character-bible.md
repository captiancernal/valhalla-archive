# Split Decision: Character Bible (v2)

*Split Decision* is a weekly first-take column about the Cleveland State Vikings, written by two fictional columnists who cannot agree on anything. Every CSU game gets one.

All characters, people, businesses and publications here are fictional. Real Cleveland neighborhoods, public places (West Side Market, Edgewater Park, the lake) and pro teams (Browns, Guardians, Cavaliers) may be mentioned in general terms, **never real players, coaches, owners or business owners by name**. Mickey and Ken share only one thing with famous TV debaters: the strength of their convictions. They never borrow a real person's catchphrases, mannerisms or biography.

v2 (approved answers, 2026-10-26): Cleveland life woven into the takes; lives move forward across 2024-2037; full recurring cast.

---

## The setup

- **The paper:** *The Lakefront Ledger*, a fictional, scrappy Cleveland daily that still prints on actual paper, out of an old building near the Flats with a freight elevator nobody trusts.
- **The arrangement:** In 2024, sports editor **Marlene Ostrowski** ran out of column inches and money in the same week. She put her two most stubborn columnists on one page under one headline, *Split Decision*, and told them to "figure it out." They have not figured it out.
- **The real origin of the feud (2014):** Ken's very first Ledger column was a polite, devastating takedown of a Mickey column predicting a Vikings bowl season. Mickey read it at 6 a.m., called the newsroom, and invited the new kid to lunch at Big Stan's deli. Ken came expecting a fight and got a corned beef on rye and a two-hour argument. **They've had lunch at Big Stan's almost every week since.** It's where most columns are really born.
- **The friendship, deep and rarely shown:** Ken eats Thanksgiving at the Bridges' house most years. Mickey is the only person Ken talks to about his father. Neither admits any of this in print. It leaks through in small moments: a held door, an extra pierogi, a "call your dad" at the end of an argument.
- **The rule they never break:** They argue about the take, never the facts. The scores, records and stats in the game facts box are gospel. They only fight about what those facts *mean*.

## The city (how Cleveland shows up)

Cleveland is the third character. Every column should feel like it happened in a real place on a real day.

- **Food and markets:** West Side Market on Saturday mornings; pierogi, kielbasa, stuffed cabbage; Polish Boys; corned beef on rye; Friday fish fries in parish halls; paczki in February; church festival potato pancakes in summer.
- **Neighborhoods:** Slavic Village (Mickey grew up there, Big Stan's is there), Old Brooklyn (Mickey lives there), Ohio City (Ken lives there; the brewpub), Tremont, Parma (Ken grew up there), Lakewood (record stores), Little Italy, the Flats, downtown.
- **Lake and weather:** Lake Erie in every mood; Edgewater at dawn (Ken runs there sometimes, mostly to brood); the wind off the water in November; lake-effect snow on a road-trip morning; the first warm day when the whole city sits outside.
- **Sports pain:** The general weight of being a Cleveland fan: decades of "wait till next year," a Browns Sunday hangover, a Guardians September, the city's collective flinch whenever something good happens. No real people named; it's the *feeling*, not the history lesson.
- **Season rhythm:** August heat and construction cones; September tailgates; October leaves on the Shoreway; November gray; December bowl trips; January in the newsroom with the radiator clanking.

## Mickey Bridges: "The Believer"

### At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Michael Francis "Mickey" Bridges |
| Age | 56 at the start of the 2024 season (born 1968) |
| Role | Senior columnist, The Lakefront Ledger (since 1994) |
| Grew up | Slavic Village, Cleveland, the third of five kids; his father worked at the steel mill, his mother at the parish rectory |
| Lives | Old Brooklyn, in the same brick double he bought in 1997 |
| Education | Cleveland State, Class of '90, communications. Never stops mentioning it. |
| Height / build | 5'9", round and sturdy, "built like a mailbox" (Ken's description) |

### Family
- **Donna Bridges:** wife of 31 years in 2024, a retired school nurse. Practical, funny, unbothered; thinks football is "fine." She's the voice of reason and has the last word at home. Went to her first game in years in 2024 (the 46-45 win at Miami (OH)).
- **Kate Bridges:** older daughter, 29 in 2024, a high school English teacher in Lakewood. Gentle, Mickey's ally, edits his columns for comma splices without being asked.
- **Bridget Bridges:** younger daughter, 26 in 2024. **Works in Ohio University's athletic department** (communications office), in Athens. She's good at her job, proud of the Bobcats, and loves her father enough to text him "sorry, Dad" after every Ohio win, which makes it worse. Mickey's crisis of loyalty is permanent and lovingly ridiculous.
- **Viking:** a beagle, Mickey's third beagle named Viking.

### His world (recurring places and people)
- **Big Stan's (Wojcik's Deli), Slavic Village:** run by his childhood friend **Stanley "Big Stan" Wojcik**, a giant, a believer louder than Mickey, who names sandwiches after Vikings stars (they get renamed when a player leaves). Lunch with Ken happens here. Stan's regulars, the "counter council" of retired steelworkers, vote on every game.
- **Sal's Barbershop, Old Brooklyn:** **Salvatore "Sal" Moretti**, born and raised in Athens, Ohio, has cut what's left of Mickey's hair for 25 years and is a die-hard Ohio Bobcats fan. Every haircut is a border war. Sal keeps a Bobcats pennant over the mirror and turns it to face Mickey after Ohio wins.
- **The Reyes family next door:** **Marco and Jess Reyes** moved from Phoenix in summer 2024 with kids **Leo (6)** and **Sofia (4)**. They'd never seen snow. Mickey is personally, systematically raising the kids as Vikings fans: a horned helmet for Leo, a "future Viking" bib for Sofia, porch-side lessons on the fight song. Marco is a good sport. Jess is suspicious of the helmets.
- **Coach Mickey:** He has coached a youth football team for 25 years, the **Our Lady of the Lake Lakers** (fictional parish team, ten- and eleven-year-olds). Half his wisdom comes from children: "a ten-year-old told me today you can't be scared of a ball. Profound."

### Psychographics
- **Core belief:** Loyalty is a virtue and doubt is a character flaw. If you're a Viking, you're family. If you're family, you get the benefit of every doubt.
- **What he wants:** To be proven right *with* the team, not instead of it.
- **What he fears:** That the good times are a dream and he'll wake up in 1999. Later: that he'll miss the good times.
- **Emotional range:** Big. Cries at bowl wins, senior days and, once, a fair catch. Never bitter for more than a day.
- **Blind spot:** He can't see a CSU player's flaws. A fumble is "the ball's fault."

### Speech patterns and voice
- **Register:** Warm, conversational, a little breathless. "Folks," "friends," "my people." He talks *to* the reader like they're on his porch.
- **Rhythm:** Long rolling sentences piled with adjectives, then a short emphatic one. "Beautiful."
- **Vocabulary:** Folksy and food-heavy. Things have "some Sunday gravy on them."
- **His own lines:** "Put it in the time capsule." "That's a Viking, folks." "Pass the pierogi." Calls the team "our guys" and the program "the family."
- **When he's losing an argument:** He brings up a heartwarming fact about a player's grandmother, or something Leo Reyes said.

### Recurring bits (rotate; never all in one column)
- **The Jersey Count:** one Vikings jersey per season covered (2024 = No. 31).
- **The Second Seat:** he gives his spare season-ticket seat to a stranger (used less from 2025 on, saved for big games).
- **The Pierogi Index:** rates wins 1-5 pierogi.
- **Sal's pennant:** which way it's facing.
- **Bridget's text:** "sorry, Dad."
- **Lakers practice wisdom.**
- **On 2035:** He refuses to discuss the 2035 national title game (M-OH 73, CLEV 70). He calls it **"The Thing"** and blames "gremlins in the machine."

### Physical description (for video)
Round friendly face with ruddy cheeks; a salt-and-pepper horseshoe of hair, mostly gone on top; bushy, expressive eyebrows that do half his talking. Light-blue eyes, crow's feet, a laugh that starts in the shoulders. Reading glasses on a beaded chain Kate made him in 2009.

### Wardrobe
Default: forest-green Vikings quarter-zip over a white collared shirt, khakis, brown walking shoes. Game days: an old green wool cardigan with a crooked felt "C" patch. Big games: a plastic Viking helmet with horns, worn indoors. Coaching: a Lakers windbreaker and a whistle he forgets to take off. Accessory: a dented "WORLD'S OKAYEST DAD" travel mug.

### Voice (for audio and video)
A warm, gravelly Cleveland baritone, slightly nasal. Fast when excited, near-whisper for the big point, laughs mid-sentence. A beloved uncle making a toast.

## "Lil" Ken Hogmanes: "The Doubter"

### At a glance
| | |
|---|---|
| Full name | Kenneth Lyle Hogmanes |
| Age | 38 at the start of the 2024 season (born 1986) |
| Role | Columnist and "numbers desk," The Lakefront Ledger (since 2014) |
| Grew up | Parma, Ohio; his grandfather "Pops" took him to his first CSU game at age 7 |
| Lives | A tidy one-bedroom in Ohio City: standing desk, two monitors, a turntable, too many records, and one fake plant "because I don't trust it to survive" |
| Education | Kent State, journalism with a statistics minor. Mickey calls this "a cry for help." |
| Height / build | 5'11" and wiry, which makes "Lil" neither accurate nor ironic. See the secret below. |

### The secret (core running gag)
**"Lil Ken Hogmanes" is a perfect anagram of "Hes gonna kill me."** Ken knows. He lives in fear that someone will notice.
- He invents a **different origin story for "Lil" every time** it comes up, and none of them match.
- If anyone gets near the truth, he will go to any length to change the subject: fake a call, fake an injury, file early, once climb out a window.
- **Never confirmed in the columns.** It's an Easter egg for sharp readers. Mickey suspects "something" but has the wrong theory: he thinks Ken was a child actor.

### Family
- **Ray Hogmanes, his father,** 72 in 2024, a retired Parma machinist who taught Ken to love the Vikings. Ray and Ken's mother, **Carol**, retired to Sarasota in 2019. **Ray has early dementia** (diagnosed 2023). Ken calls him every Sunday about the game. Some weeks Ray knows exactly who Coach Dolan is; some weeks he asks how the 1990s team is doing. **Used sparingly and with care:** a line or two, a few times a season, never played for laughs, never for cheap tears. It's the quiet reason Ken holds on so tight.
- **Carol Hogmanes:** his mother, practical, carries the weight; gets on the Sunday calls to say "he's having a good day" or not.
- **Dana Hogmanes:** his sister in Columbus; texts "relax" during every game.
- **"Pops" Hogmanes:** his late grandfather. "Never trust a lead." The only advice Ken has ever taken.

### His world (recurring places and people)
- **The Lantern Room, Ohio City:** a fictional brewpub near his apartment. Ken runs a Tuesday trivia team, **Regression to the Mean**, which has never won. The bartender, **Nadia Petrakis**, is a **Miami (OH) alum** who taunts him professionally, keeps a RedHawks bobblehead next to the taps, and pours him "the sad IPA" after losses.
- **Lunch at Big Stan's** with Mickey, weekly since 2014. Ken always orders the same thing and always complains about it.
- **Spin Cycle Records, Lakewood:** a fictional record store where he spends money he shouldn't. He plays sad jazz after losses and very slightly less sad jazz after wins; the album choice is a mood report.
- **Romance (slow burn):** In 2027 he meets **Teresa Kowalczyk**, a night-shift ER nurse who doesn't care about football at all, at Spin Cycle Records (they reached for the same record). They date, move in together (2029), get engaged (2031) and marry (2032). Teresa is warm, unimpressed by sports and amused by him; she's the person who finally makes his doubt feel less necessary. She calls the Doom Model "your little spreadsheet friend."

### Psychographics
- **Core belief:** Hope is a loan, and the interest is brutal. He won't believe until the trophy is in the case. Even then, he'd like to check the engraving.
- **Why:** Scar tissue. He grew up a CSU fan in the bad years and built a wall out of spreadsheets. **He's secretly the biggest Vikings fan in the building.**
- **What he fears:** Getting his hopes up, and losing things he can't get back (his father's memory is the real version of this; football is the safe version).
- **Tell:** After a big win he writes one sentence that is accidentally beautiful, then catches himself.

### Speech patterns and voice
- **Register:** Dry, precise, clipped. Short declarative sentences. He lowers the temperature.
- **Vocabulary:** Wide and slightly showy: "specious," "pyrrhic," "load-bearing," "vibes-based." Loves a semicolon.
- **His own lines:** "Put it in the case, then we'll talk." "Respectfully, no." "That's a vibe, not a fact." "Ask me in January."
- **Humor:** Deadpan understatement. CSU wins by 50: "Competent."
- **When he's losing an argument:** He cites a fact from the game box that technically supports him and misses the point.

### Recurring bits (rotate)
- The "Lil" Origin of the Week (never the true one).
- **The Doom Model:** his projection spreadsheet. It predicts disaster with a 100% success rate "eventually."
- **The Case:** he keeps raising the bar for belief.
- **Nadia's bobblehead** and the sad IPA.
- **The record on the turntable** as a mood report.
- **Grudging respect for Ohio:** OHIO's five straight titles (2029-2033) are his horror film. He respects Coach Slocombe's Bobcats the way you respect weather.
- **Miami (OH):** He calls their offense "a slot machine that pays out."
- **2035:** He brings up "The Thing" when Mickey gets too happy, then quietly agrees it was gremlins.

### Physical description (for video)
Lean, angular face; dark hair in a neat side part that one cowlick defeats every afternoon; a close-trimmed beard he starts in 2030 "for gravitas." Dark-brown eyes, permanent half-squint of skepticism, one eyebrow higher than the other at rest. Thin black rectangular glasses he pushes up with one finger before saying "Respectfully, no."

### Wardrobe
Default: charcoal crewneck sweater over a pale-blue oxford, dark jeans, clean white sneakers. Game days: plus a forest-green Vikings beanie "for warmth." Big games: his grandfather's vintage CSU windbreaker, which he claims is "ironic" and is not. Accessory: a dented thermos of black coffee and a pocket notebook with a tiny pencil.

### Voice (for audio and video)
A dry, mid-range tenor with crisp consonants. Even and quick, with long pointed pauses before punchlines. A public-radio host who has had enough.

## Writing the columns (how the world gets in)

- **Weave, don't stage.** No separate scene section. Each take pulls the week's argument from somewhere in their lives: what Big Stan's counter council said, what Sal muttered with the clippers, what Leo Reyes asked on the porch, what Nadia poured, what record Ken put on, what Ray said on Sunday.
- **Make every column unique.** Use one or two world details per take, rotating places and people. Don't repeat the same pairing two weeks in a row. Not every bit, every week.
- **Let the game set the scene.** Road games bring weather and travel; rivalry weeks bring Sal, Bridget and Nadia; big wins bring the deli; hard losses bring Ken's records and, rarely, his father.
- **Time moves.** Follow the timeline. Characters age, kids grow, Ken's life fills up. Mention life events in the season they happen, and refer back to them later.
- **Facts stay facts.** Scores, records, opponents, players and season stats come only from the data. Life color can be invented but never contradicts the data. No invented per-game player stats.

## Column rules (unchanged)

1. **Format:** headline; **game facts box** (generated from data); **Mickey's take** (about 120-170 words); **Ken's rebuttal** (about 120-170 words); **one back-and-forth exchange** (3-6 short lines); **Final Verdict** (one line). Total about 300-450 words.
2. **Facts come only from the Site Database.** No invented stats, plays, or quotes from real people.
3. **Color can be invented but never contradicts the data.**
4. **Players** can be praised or doubted by name using only season-level facts (season leaders, totals, awards, All-Americans, ratings) and the game result. Season totals appear only in a season's final column.
5. **Clean.** No profanity, no slurs, no crude jokes. College-bar humor means wit, not filth.
6. **Rivals:** Friendly-but-pointed jabs at Ohio (Coach Tim Slocombe) and Miami (OH) (Coach Joe Snyderwine) are welcome. Punch at the programs and football choices, never at the real people behind them.
7. **No real-person impressions,** catchphrases or quotes.
8. **Continuity:** follow the timeline and the continuity log.
